Deployable Continuum Robot for Space Applications

A TRL-3 prototype of a deployable continuum robot designed for space station inspection tasks

See Full Design Report

What are continuum robots?

Continuum robots are defined by a continuous, flexible body that technically offers the robot infinite degrees of freedom along its body. This is in contrast with tradition rigid link robotics, which possess rigid links that are connected and actuated thru joint mechanisms; these robot posses a finite amount of degrees of freedom.

Problem statement

The team was tasked with creating a proof-of-concept, origami-inspired continuum robot aimed at alleviating inspection and maintenance tasks onboard a space station, which are traditionally costly, dangerous, and unreliable. The robot shall perform a pick-and-place operation in a 100-mm taskspace over 30 times in less than 30 seconds, for an object weighing no less than 150 grams. The robot itself, when stowed, shall conform to 3U CubeSat standard, compress to 50% of its original length, and weigh less than 1.5 kg. The total robot workspace shall be a 200-mm hemisphere.

Results

Each design criterion was met, except for the compression ratio, which only reached 60%. The final design solution is a tendon-driven, two-module continuum robot with a spring backbone separated by PLA disks and controlled by pulleys operated by stepper motors. The end-effector is a two-prong TPU compliant pincher controlled by a DC motor to perform the pick-and-place operation. The origami is divided into panels affixed to each face of each segment (six segments in total, three per module). Each panel is composed of Kapton film and PLA rigid tabs to guide the robot motion. The robot is completed for translation to research, through which improvements to fidelity and alterations for in-space use are possible. For example, the team recommends implementing inverse kinematics for absolute-positioning and sensor-based navigation, as well as characterizing and validating additional origami geometries.
