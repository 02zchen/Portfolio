Minerva

A 5U deployable Cubesat featuring guided recovery, a gimbal camera, and bush avoidance algorithms. Part of the 2024-2025 Duke Aero Rocket, Perseus.

See CAD See Full Report

Background

Duke Aero is the premier high-powered rocketry team at Duke University that competes at IREC each June. For the 2024-2025 season, Duke Aero designed, fabricated, tested, and launched a Student Research and Developed (SRAD) solid fueled vehicle that flew in the 30K category - Perseus. Teams competing within IREC can opt into participating into the SDL Payload Challenge, in which the vehicles fly with an SRAD Cubesat (an industry class of Nanosatellites) that can demonstrate some form of scientific or technical experiment.

Minerva

Minerva is a deployable, demonstration of technology payload that follows the 5U CubeSat standard. The payload is separated into 4 compartments by 3 bulkheads. The leftmost compartment contains a spring loaded parachute plate, which stores a 7.5 ft cruciform parachute (not shown) while Minerva is within the rocket. A hex shaft connects the parachute plate to the recovery deployment mechanism, which controls when the spring plate is released and is located in the compartment second from the left. The second compartment also contains a guided recovery mechanism with two 5 turn servos and pulleys to control the rigging of the cruciform chute. Certain riggings will induce parachute spin and glide towards a predetermined landing location. The third compartment will house most of the electronics, including flight computers, batteries, antennas, and transmitters. The last compartment stores a 2 degree-of-freedom gimbal camera system enclosed in a clear acrylic case, designed to assist with the onboard computer vision capabilities by counteracting roll.
